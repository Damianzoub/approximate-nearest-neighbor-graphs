# Space Exploration

Space exploration encompasses the discovery and study of outer space through the use of astronomy, robotic spacecraft, and human spaceflight. From the first Sputnik satellite in 1957 to the ongoing missions to Mars and the Moon, space exploration has driven technological innovation, expanded scientific knowledge, and inspired generations.

## The History of Human Spaceflight

The Space Race between the United States and Soviet Union in the 1950s and 60s accelerated space exploration dramatically. Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space in 1961. The Apollo programme culminated in the first crewed Moon landing on July 20, 1969, when Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the lunar surface. Six Apollo missions successfully landed on the Moon between 1969 and 1972.

The Space Shuttle programme (1981-2011) demonstrated reusable spacecraft and enabled construction of the International Space Station (ISS). The ISS, continuously inhabited since 2000, serves as a laboratory for science in microgravity, medical research, and technology demonstration, and as a symbol of international cooperation.

## Mars Exploration

Mars is the primary target for robotic and eventual human exploration. NASA's Perseverance rover, which landed in Jezero Crater in February 2021, is searching for signs of ancient microbial life and collecting rock samples for future return to Earth. The Ingenuity helicopter, deployed by Perseverance, became the first powered aircraft to fly on another planet.

China's Tianwen-1 mission successfully delivered the Zhurong rover to Mars in 2021. The UAE's Hope orbiter began studying Martian atmosphere and weather. NASA and ESA are collaborating on Mars Sample Return, an ambitious mission to bring Perseverance's samples back to Earth in the 2030s.

## Commercial Spaceflight

The commercialisation of space has transformed the industry. SpaceX's Falcon 9 rocket, featuring reusable first-stage boosters, dramatically reduced launch costs. The Crew Dragon spacecraft now routinely transports astronauts to the ISS. SpaceX's Starship — a fully reusable super-heavy-lift launch vehicle — aims to enable missions to the Moon, Mars, and beyond at costs orders of magnitude lower than previous systems.

Blue Origin, Rocket Lab, and United Launch Alliance are developing next-generation launch vehicles. Companies like Planet Labs operate large constellations of Earth observation satellites for agriculture, disaster response, and environmental monitoring. The satellite internet industry, led by SpaceX's Starlink and Amazon's Project Kuiper, aims to provide global broadband coverage.

## The Artemis Programme

NASA's Artemis programme aims to return humans to the Moon by the mid-2020s, this time to stay. Artemis includes the Space Launch System (SLS) rocket, Orion spacecraft, and the Gateway lunar orbit station. The programme plans to land the first woman and first person of colour on the Moon, establishing a sustainable presence to develop technologies for Mars missions.

International partners including ESA, JAXA, and the Canadian Space Agency are contributing to Artemis through hardware, crew participation, and lunar surface operations.

## The Search for Extraterrestrial Life

Astrobiology studies the potential for life beyond Earth. The discovery of exoplanets — planets orbiting other stars — using missions like Kepler and TESS has revealed that potentially habitable worlds are common in the galaxy. The James Webb Space Telescope can characterise exoplanet atmospheres, searching for biosignatures such as oxygen, methane, and water vapour in combinations that might indicate biological activity.

Within our solar system, icy moons such as Europa (Jupiter) and Enceladus (Saturn) are considered prime candidates for extraterrestrial life, harbouring liquid water oceans beneath their icy surfaces.
