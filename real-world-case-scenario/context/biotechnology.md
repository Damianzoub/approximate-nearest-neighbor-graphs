# Biotechnology

Biotechnology applies biological systems, living organisms, or their derivatives to develop products and processes that improve human life and the health of the planet. Modern biotechnology spans genomics, genetic engineering, synthetic biology, and bioinformatics, with applications ranging from medicine and agriculture to environmental remediation.

## CRISPR-Cas9 Gene Editing

The discovery of CRISPR-Cas9 as a precise gene editing tool transformed biology. CRISPR (Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats) is a natural bacterial immune system repurposed as a molecular scissors that can cut DNA at specific sequences guided by a short RNA molecule. Scientists can then disable a gene, repair a mutation, or insert new genetic material.

Clinical trials using CRISPR are underway for sickle cell disease, beta-thalassemia, and certain cancers. In 2023, the first CRISPR-based therapy — Casgevy — was approved for sickle cell disease, marking a historic milestone. CRISPR agriculture applications include disease-resistant crops and livestock with improved traits, raising productivity while reducing pesticide use.

## mRNA Technology

The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated the development and deployment of messenger RNA (mRNA) vaccines. Rather than injecting a weakened pathogen or protein, mRNA vaccines deliver genetic instructions for cells to produce a viral protein, triggering an immune response. The rapid development of mRNA COVID-19 vaccines demonstrated the platform's speed and flexibility.

Beyond vaccines, mRNA technology is being developed for cancer immunotherapy — personalised cancer vaccines that target mutations unique to an individual's tumour — and treatments for rare genetic disorders. Companies like Moderna and BioNTech are expanding their mRNA pipelines across infectious diseases, oncology, and cardiovascular conditions.

## Protein Engineering and AlphaFold

Proteins are the molecular machines of life, performing virtually every biological function. Understanding how a protein's amino acid sequence determines its three-dimensional structure — the protein folding problem — was one of biology's grand challenges. DeepMind's AlphaFold2, released in 2021, achieved near-experimental accuracy in protein structure prediction, solving a 50-year-old problem.

AlphaFold has been used to predict structures for virtually all known proteins, accelerating drug discovery by revealing potential drug binding sites and enabling the design of novel enzymes and protein therapeutics. Computational protein design tools allow scientists to engineer proteins with entirely new functions not found in nature.

## Synthetic Biology

Synthetic biology treats living organisms as programmable systems, applying engineering principles to design and construct new biological parts, devices, and organisms. Applications include engineering microorganisms to produce biofuels, biodegradable plastics, flavours, fragrances, and pharmaceuticals. Yeast strains have been engineered to produce artemisinin — an anti-malarial drug — at scale and low cost.

Cell-free systems — containing cellular machinery but no living cells — enable rapid prototyping of biological circuits and production of proteins without the constraints of cellular metabolism.

## Ethical Dimensions

Biotechnology raises profound ethical questions. Germline editing — modifying DNA in eggs, sperm, or embryos — raises concerns about heritable changes and consent. The creation of synthetic organisms and the dual-use potential of biological research for bioweapons require robust governance frameworks. Equitable access to genetic medicines, which can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars per patient, is a pressing global health justice issue.
